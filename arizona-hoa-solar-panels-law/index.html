Can Your Arizona HOA Stop You From Installing Solar?

Short answer: an Arizona HOA can't prohibit solar, but it can have reasonable placement rules.

HOAs can't prohibit solar

Notwithstanding any provision in the community documents, an association shall not prohibit the installation or use of a solar energy device as defined in A.R.S. 44-1761 (A.R.S. 33-1816(A)).

What rules are allowed

An HOA may adopt reasonable rules about placement, but only if they don't prevent installation, impair the device's functioning, restrict its use, or adversely affect its cost or efficiency (A.R.S. 33-1816(B)).

Attorney fees

In a court action against the board for violating this section, the court shall award reasonable attorney fees and costs to any party who substantially prevails (A.R.S. 33-1816(C)). That cuts both ways, so it's worth trying to resolve things on paper first.

Practical steps

  1. Read your HOA's architectural rules and submit any required application.
  2. If a placement rule would hurt production or raise cost, ask your installer for a written comparison.
  3. Respond to a denial in writing, citing 33-1816(B) and attaching that comparison.
  4. Request the architectural committee's records on your application (A.R.S. 33-1805).

Step by step

  1. Read your architectural guidelines for solar placement and application rules.
  2. Get a written design from your installer showing production at the preferred location.
  3. Submit a complete application with plans and the installer's design.
  4. If a placement rule is proposed, ask your installer to put in writing any loss of efficiency or added cost.
  5. Respond to a denial or condition in writing and ask for the reasons.

Sample wording

Thank you for reviewing my solar application. Under A.R.S. 33-1816, an association may adopt reasonable placement rules only if they do not prevent installation, impair functioning, restrict use, or adversely affect cost or efficiency. My installer's attached analysis shows the required placement would [reduce production / increase cost]. I ask the committee to approve the original layout.

Common mistakes

  • Installing first and asking later. Skipping a required application can create a separate violation.
  • Assuming any rule is illegal. Reasonable placement rules are allowed.
  • Leaving it verbal. Get approvals and conditions in writing.

Why written records matter here

Because a court awards attorney fees to whichever party substantially prevails in a 33-1816 case, a clear written file showing what was requested, what the installer said, and what the HOA decided helps everyone see the dispute accurately and may make it easier to settle without going that far.

What counts as a solar energy device

The protection in 33-1816 points to the definition of "solar energy device" in A.R.S. 44-1761. If you are installing something unusual, such as a ground mount, a pergola-mounted array, or solar water heating, read that definition with your installer and include it in your application so the committee is working from the same language.

Questions to ask before you sign an installation contract

  • Does the HOA require architectural approval before installation, and how long does review usually take?
  • Will the installer prepare drawings showing panel placement and wiring for the application?
  • If the HOA asks for a different placement, will the installer document any production or cost difference in writing?

If you live in a condo

We did not find a solar provision in the Arizona Condominium Act matching 33-1816, which applies to planned communities. Condo roofs are often common elements controlled by the association, so condo owners should check their declaration and rules, and ask the association in writing about its process.

FAQ

Can my Arizona HOA deny my solar panels?

It can't prohibit a solar energy device, though it may adopt reasonable placement rules (A.R.S. 33-1816).

Can the HOA make me put panels on the back roof?

Only if the rule doesn't impair function or adversely affect cost or efficiency (A.R.S. 33-1816(B)).

Do I still need HOA approval?

Your documents may require an application; placement rules are allowed if reasonable (A.R.S. 33-1816(B)).
