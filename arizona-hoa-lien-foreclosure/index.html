HOA Liens and Foreclosure in Arizona

The assessment lien

An Arizona planned-community association has a common expense lien on a property for any assessment from the time it becomes due (A.R.S. 33-1807(A)).

When foreclosure is allowed

The lien may be foreclosed like a mortgage, but only if the owner has been and remains delinquent for 18 months or in the amount of $10,000 or more, whichever comes first, measured on the filing date. For a special assessment with an initial value of $10,000 or more, only the 18-month threshold applies (A.R.S. 33-1807(A)).

Payment plan effort first

Before filing a foreclosure action, the board must make reasonable efforts to communicate with the member and offer a reasonable payment plan (A.R.S. 33-1807(A)).

Fines and "member expenses" are different

Member expenses aren't enforceable as common expense liens. The HOA can get a judgment lien for them only after a court judgment is entered and recorded; that judgment lien can't be foreclosed and is effective only on conveyance of an interest in the property (A.R.S. 33-1807(B)).

Priority

The lien is generally prior to other liens, except liens recorded before the declaration, a recorded first mortgage or first deed of trust (and certain first contracts for sale), and real estate taxes and governmental assessments (A.R.S. 33-1807(C)).

If you're behind

  1. Ask in writing for a ledger of what you owe.
  2. Check that late charges follow A.R.S. 33-1803(A).
  3. Ask about a payment plan in writing, and keep copies.

Step by step if you're behind

  1. Request an itemized ledger separating assessments, late charges and other charges.
  2. Separate assessments from member expenses. Fines and other member expenses aren't common expense liens.
  3. Propose a realistic payment plan in writing.
  4. Keep paying current assessments if you can, so the balance stops growing.
  5. Get any agreement in writing and keep proof of every payment.
  6. If you get a foreclosure filing, consider talking with an Arizona attorney promptly.

Sample wording

Please send an itemized ledger for [address] showing assessments, late charges and any other charges separately. I would like to set up a payment plan and propose [amount] per [period] starting [date]. Please confirm in writing.

Common mistakes

  • Ignoring letters. Collection costs and charges can grow.
  • Assuming a fine can lead to foreclosure. Under 33-1807(B), a judgment lien for member expenses can't be foreclosed.
  • Paying with no record. Always get receipts.

Understanding where a lien stands

A lien is a legal claim against the property, not a demand to leave it. For most owners the practical questions are how much is owed, whether it is an assessment or another kind of charge, and how long it has gone unpaid. Those three facts decide whether the foreclosure thresholds in 33-1807(A) are even in play. If you sell or refinance, the lien will usually come up at closing, so dealing with it early tends to be simpler than at the last minute.

What to keep in your file

  • Every statement and collection letter, in date order
  • Proof of each payment
  • Your written payment-plan proposals and the HOA's replies
  • Any notices about late charges, with dates

If you live in a condo

A.R.S. 33-1256 sets the same rules for condo units: a common expense lien from when an assessment is due, foreclosure only after 18 months of delinquency or $10,000 or more (18 months only for special assessments of $10,000 or more), reasonable efforts to offer a payment plan first, and unit owner expenses are not common expense liens; a judgment lien for them can't be foreclosed (33-1256(A),(B)).

FAQ

Can my Arizona HOA foreclose for unpaid dues?

Only after 18 months of delinquency or $10,000 or more owed, whichever is first (A.R.S. 33-1807(A)).

Can an HOA foreclose over fines?

Member expenses aren't common expense liens; a judgment lien for them can't be foreclosed (A.R.S. 33-1807(B)).

Does the HOA have to offer a payment plan?

The board must make reasonable efforts to offer one before filing foreclosure (A.R.S. 33-1807(A)).
