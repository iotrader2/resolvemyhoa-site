Arizona HOA Fines, Late Fees, and Dues Increases: What the Law Limits

Arizona doesn't let planned-community HOAs set fees with no limits at all. A.R.S. 33-1803 sets several guardrails.

Late charges on assessments

  • A payment is late if unpaid 15 or more days after its due date, unless your documents allow longer.
  • Late charges are capped at the greater of $15 or 10% of the unpaid assessment.
  • They can be imposed only after the HOA gives notice that the assessment is overdue (or will be considered overdue after a certain date).
  • Your payments go first to the unpaid principal, then to interest. (A.R.S. 33-1803(A))

Fines (penalties) for rule violations

  • The board may impose reasonable penalties only after notice and an opportunity to be heard.
  • A late charge on an unpaid penalty can't exceed the greater of $15 or 10% of the unpaid penalty, regardless of what the documents say.
  • The notice must include information on how the penalty will be enforced. (A.R.S. 33-1803(B))

The statute doesn't set a dollar cap on the fine itself; it requires that penalties be reasonable and follow notice and a hearing opportunity.

Regular assessment increases

Unless your documents set a lower limit, the HOA can't raise the regular assessment more than 20% over the prior fiscal year's without approval of a majority of members (A.R.S. 33-1803(A)).

What to do with a fine you dispute

  1. Ask in writing for the rule, the date, and the observer (see our violation-letter guide).
  2. Ask for your opportunity to be heard if you didn't get one.
  3. Request records showing how the fine was decided (A.R.S. 33-1805).
  4. If unresolved, consider an ADRE petition (A.R.S. 32-2199.01).

Step by step: checking a fine or late charge

  1. Get your ledger in writing and match each charge to a notice.
  2. For late charges, check the due date and the 15-day rule, then compare the charge with the greater of $15 or 10% of the unpaid amount.
  3. For fines, confirm you got notice and an opportunity to be heard before the penalty was imposed.
  4. Check how payments were applied: principal first, then interest.
  5. Put your dispute in writing and keep paying undisputed assessments so the dispute stays narrow.

Sample wording

I am requesting an itemized ledger for my account at [address]. For the penalty dated [date], please identify the rule, the notice I was sent, and when I was given an opportunity to be heard as required by A.R.S. 33-1803(B). I also ask that the late charges be reviewed against the limits in A.R.S. 33-1803.

Common mistakes

  • Withholding all dues to protest a fine. That can create a separate, larger problem.
  • Assuming every fee is illegal. The statute allows reasonable penalties; the question is process and limits.
  • Not asking for the hearing you're entitled to before a penalty.

If you live in a condo

Under A.R.S. 33-1242(A)(11), a condo association may impose late charges only after notice that the assessment is overdue (or will be considered overdue after a certain date), and may impose reasonable monetary penalties only after notice and an opportunity to be heard. The $15 / 10% late-charge cap and the 20% assessment-increase limit described above come from the planned-community statute (33-1803); we have not confirmed matching condo language in 33-1242, so check your condominium documents and the Condominium Act before relying on them.

FAQ

Is there a limit on HOA late fees in Arizona?

For planned communities, late charges are limited to the greater of $15 or 10% of the unpaid amount (A.R.S. 33-1803(A),(B)).

Can my HOA raise dues as much as it wants?

Not the regular assessment: more than a 20% increase over the prior year needs majority member approval, unless your documents set a lower limit (A.R.S. 33-1803(A)).

Can an HOA fine me without warning?

Penalties require notice and an opportunity to be heard (A.R.S. 33-1803(B)).
