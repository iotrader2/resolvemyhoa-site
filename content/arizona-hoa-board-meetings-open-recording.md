# Arizona HOA Board Meetings: Attending, Speaking, and Recording

Decisions about your dues and rules happen at board meetings. In Arizona, those meetings are generally open to you.

## Meetings are open to members
Under A.R.S. 33-1804(A), all meetings of the members' association and the board, and regularly scheduled committee meetings, are open to all members or their written designees, notwithstanding contrary documents.

## You can speak
The board may set reasonable time limits, but must let a member speak **once after the board discusses a specific agenda item and before it takes formal action** on it, in addition to other opportunities, and allow a reasonable number of speakers on each side (A.R.S. 33-1804(A)).

## You can record
Attendees may audiotape or videotape the open portions of board and member meetings. The board **can't require advance notice** of recording and may adopt reasonable rules, but those rules can't preclude recording, unless the board itself records and makes unedited recordings available to members on request without restrictions on use as evidence. If the board records an open meeting, it must keep a copy for at least six months and provide the unedited recording on request (A.R.S. 33-1804(A)).

## When a meeting can be closed
A portion may be closed only for consideration **without action** of limited topics, including legal advice from the association's attorney, pending or contemplated litigation, personal/health/financial information about an individual member or employee, employee job-performance matters, and a member's appeal of a violation or penalty (unless that member asks for it to be open) (A.R.S. 33-1804(A)).

## Notice
After declarant control ends, members must get notice of board meetings and agendas at least **48 hours** in advance by newsletter, conspicuous posting, or other reasonable means (A.R.S. 33-1804).

## Practical tips
Bring a written statement, stick to the agenda item, and request the minutes afterward (A.R.S. 33-1805).

## Step by step: before, during and after
1. **Watch for the notice and agenda** (at least 48 hours ahead after declarant control ends).
2. **Pick the agenda item** you care about and write a two-minute statement.
3. **Bring your phone to record** if you want; no advance notice is required.
4. **Speak after the board discusses the item** and before it votes.
5. **Request the minutes**, and the recording if the board made one, afterward.

## Sample wording for a statement
> My name is [name] at [address]. On item [#], I ask the board to [specific request] because [one or two facts]. I have provided written details to the management company.

## Common mistakes
- **Raising off-agenda issues.** Ask for them to be put on a future agenda instead.
- **Treating a closed session as secret voting.** Closed portions in planned communities are for consideration without action.
- **Not keeping your own notes** of what was decided.

## If the board won't let you speak or record
Write to the board afterward, describe what happened with the date and agenda item, cite 33-1804(A), and ask that the issue be fixed at the next meeting. Keep a copy. Repeated problems may be something you can raise in an ADRE petition.

## If you live in a condo
A.R.S. 33-1248 applies the same open-meeting rules to condos: meetings are open to members, members may speak once after board discussion and before action, attendees may record open portions without advance notice, and notice of board meetings is at least 48 hours. One wording difference: the condo statute says closed portions are limited to "consideration of" listed topics, without the planned-community phrase "without action."

## FAQ

**Can I record my HOA board meeting in Arizona?**
Yes, attendees may audio or video record open portions, and the board can't require advance notice (A.R.S. 33-1804(A)).

**Do I get to speak at HOA meetings?**
Members may speak at least once after board discussion of an agenda item and before formal action (A.R.S. 33-1804(A)).

**How much notice must the HOA give?**
After declarant control ends, at least 48 hours for board meetings (A.R.S. 33-1804).
