Flags and Political Signs in Arizona HOAs

Flags an HOA can't prohibit

Under A.R.S. 33-1808(A), notwithstanding the community documents, an HOA can't prohibit front- or backyard display of, among others: the American flag or a U.S. uniformed services flag (displayed consistent with the federal flag code), the POW/MIA flag, the Arizona state flag, an Arizona Indian nations flag, the Gadsden flag, a first responder flag, a blue star or gold star service flag, and historic versions of the American flag. See the statute for the full list.

Rules the HOA may set on flags

The HOA must adopt reasonable placement rules and may (A.R.S. 33-1808(B)): regulate flagpole location and size; limit you to two wall-mounted holders; limit you to two flags at once; and limit flagpole height to your home's rooftop. It can't prohibit a flagpole in your front or back yard.

Political signs

An HOA can't prohibit indoor or outdoor display of a political sign on your property, except it may prohibit signs (A.R.S. 33-1808(C)):

  • earlier than 71 days before a primary election;
  • later than 15 days after the general election;
  • for a candidate who doesn't advance, later than 15 days after the primary.

Size and number

The HOA may regulate size and number if no more restrictive than local city, town, or county rules. If the locality doesn't regulate, the HOA can't limit the number, but total dimensions of all political signs may be capped at nine square feet (A.R.S. 33-1808(D)).

Got a notice about a flag or sign?

Respond in writing within 21 days (A.R.S. 33-1803(C)) and ask which specific rule applies.

Step by step

  1. Identify what you're displaying and whether it's on the protected list.
  2. Read your HOA's placement rules for flags and signs.
  3. Check election dates to know when the political-sign window opens and closes.
  4. Check your city or county sign rules, since HOA size and number limits can't be stricter.
  5. Answer any notice in writing within 21 days.

Sample wording

I am responding under A.R.S. 33-1803 to the notice dated [date] about my [flag/sign]. A.R.S. 33-1808 provides that an association shall not prohibit [this flag / this political sign during the allowed period]. Please identify the specific rule you believe I am violating and whether your concern is placement, size or number.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming any flag is protected. Only the listed flags are covered by 33-1808(A).
  • Leaving political signs up too long after the election window closes.
  • Ignoring size limits or more than two flags at once if your HOA has adopted those rules.

For sale and rental signs

The same statute also covers real estate signs. If you are selling or renting, read the "for sale, rent or lease signs" provisions in 33-1808 before placing a sign, and check what size and type of sign it allows.

If you live in a condo

A.R.S. 33-1261 protects outdoor display of the same list of flags. Condo associations may regulate flagpole location and size but may not prohibit installing a flagpole (33-1261(B)). Political signs may be placed on your unit and limited common elements that touch the unit, such as doors, walls or patios, other than the roof, with the same 71-day / 15-day windows (33-1261(E)). Size and number rules can't be stricter than local ordinances; if none apply, the total is capped at nine square feet (33-1261(F)).

FAQ

Can my Arizona HOA ban political signs?

Not within the protected window; it may prohibit them earlier than 71 days before a primary or later than 15 days after the general election (A.R.S. 33-1808(C)).

Can my HOA ban a flagpole?

It can't prohibit a front- or backyard flagpole but may regulate location, size, and height (A.R.S. 33-1808(B)).

How big can political signs be?

If your locality doesn't regulate, the HOA may cap total sign area at nine square feet (A.R.S. 33-1808(D)).
