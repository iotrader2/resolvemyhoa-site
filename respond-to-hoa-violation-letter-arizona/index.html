How to Respond to an HOA Violation Letter in Arizona

A violation letter from your HOA can feel like an accusation. In Arizona, though, the law gives you a specific, written way to respond, and it asks the HOA to answer you with specifics.

You can send a written response within 21 days

Under A.R.S. 33-1803(C), if you get a written notice that your property violates the community documents, you may send the association a written response by certified mail within 21 calendar days after the date of the notice. Send it to the address shown in the notice. This applies whether or not the notice includes a fine.

Tips:

  • Count from the date printed on the notice, not the day you opened it.
  • Keep your certified-mail receipt and a copy of everything you send.
  • Stay factual and calm: what you were told, what you've done, and what you're asking for.

What the HOA has to send back

Within 10 business days after receiving your certified response, the association must reply in writing with at least the following, unless the original notice already included it (A.R.S. 33-1803(D)):

  1. The provision of the community documents allegedly violated.
  2. The date of the violation, or the date it was observed.
  3. The first and last name of the person or persons who observed it.
  4. The process you must follow to contest the notice.

Enforcement pauses during that exchange

Unless the original notice already explained how to contest it, the HOA may not proceed with enforcement, including collecting attorney fees, before or during that information exchange, and it must give you written notice of your option to petition for an administrative hearing with the Arizona Department of Real Estate (A.R.S. 33-1803(E)).

Fines need notice and a chance to be heard

The board may impose reasonable monetary penalties only after notice and an opportunity to be heard (A.R.S. 33-1803(B)).

If it doesn't settle

At any time, before or after the information exchange, you may petition for a hearing under A.R.S. 32-2199.01 if the dispute is within the Department of Real Estate's jurisdiction (A.R.S. 33-1803(E)). See our guide to the ADRE HOA petition.

A simple response outline

  • Your name, address, and the notice date.
  • A statement that you are responding under A.R.S. 33-1803.
  • Your facts (fixed, disputed, or need clarification), with photos if helpful.
  • A request for the information listed in 33-1803(D) if it's missing.

Step by step: the first week

  1. Write down the notice date and count 21 calendar days forward. Put that date on your calendar.
  2. Photograph the area the notice describes, with the date visible if possible.
  3. Find the rule. Look up the cited section of your CC&Rs or rules. If no rule is cited, that is the first thing to ask for.
  4. Decide your position: already fixed, will fix by a certain date, disagree, or need more information.
  5. Send your reply by certified mail to the address in the notice and keep the receipt.

Sample wording

Re: Notice dated [date], [your address]

I am responding under A.R.S. 33-1803 to the notice above. [The item has been corrected as of (date); photos attached.] / [I do not believe this is a violation because (reason).] Unless already provided, please send me the provision allegedly violated, the date of the violation or observation, the first and last name of the person who observed it, and the process to contest the notice.

Sincerely, [name]

Common mistakes

  • Replying only by phone or email. The 21-day response in 33-1803(C) is a certified-mail process.
  • Arguing, or admitting more than you need to. Keep it short and factual.
  • Missing the window because you counted from when you opened the letter.
  • Ignoring a follow-up because you assumed a phone call settled it. Get the closure in writing.

If you live in a condo

Arizona condominiums follow the same process under A.R.S. 33-1242(B)–(D): a 21-calendar-day certified-mail response, a 10-business-day written reply with the same four items, and the same pause on enforcement and notice of your ADRE petition option. Condo associations also have a statutory "duty to act reasonably," which includes exercising discretionary powers neutrally, fairly, without favoritism and in a nonarbitrary way (A.R.S. 33-1242(E)).

FAQ

How long do I have to respond to an HOA violation letter in Arizona?

You may send a written response by certified mail within 21 calendar days after the date of the notice (A.R.S. 33-1803(C)).

How long does the HOA have to answer?

Within 10 business days after receiving your certified-mail response (A.R.S. 33-1803(D)).

Does the HOA have to tell me who reported the violation?

The reply must include the first and last name of the person or persons who observed the violation, unless the original notice already did (A.R.S. 33-1803(D)(3)).

Can my HOA fine me without a hearing?

Penalties may be imposed only after notice and an opportunity to be heard (A.R.S. 33-1803(B)).
