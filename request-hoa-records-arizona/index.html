How to Request Your HOA's Records in Arizona

Budgets, contracts, invoices, minutes, violation files: records are often the fastest way to understand a dispute. Arizona law gives members a right to see them.

Your right to examine records

Under A.R.S. 33-1805(A), all financial and other records of the association must be made reasonably available for examination by any member, or by a person the member designates in writing.

  • No charge to review records.
  • The HOA has 10 business days to fulfill a request to examine.
  • For copies, the HOA has 10 business days, and may charge no more than 15 cents per page.

What can be withheld

The HOA may withhold portions relating to (A.R.S. 33-1805(B)):

  1. Privileged attorney-association communications.
  2. Pending litigation.
  3. Minutes or records of board sessions that are allowed to be closed under 33-1804.
  4. Personal, health, or financial records of an individual member or employee (or contractor employee).
  5. Job performance, compensation, health records, or specific complaints about an individual employee.

It also need not disclose records if doing so would violate state or federal law (33-1805(C)).

How to write the request

  • Send it in writing and keep proof (certified mail or email with a saved copy).
  • Name specific records and a date range, e.g., "the 2026 budget, the landscaping contract, and work orders for my street since January."
  • Say whether you want to examine them or buy copies.
  • Note the date: 10 business days is your benchmark.

If the HOA doesn't respond

Follow up in writing. A persistent refusal to follow the statute may be something you can raise in an ADRE petition (A.R.S. 32-2199.01).

Step by step

  1. List exactly what you want and why it matters to your issue.
  2. Choose examine or copy. Reviewing is free; copies may cost up to 15 cents per page.
  3. Send a dated written request and keep proof.
  4. Calendar 10 business days.
  5. Review and take notes, then request copies only of what you need.

Sample wording

Under A.R.S. 33-1805, I request to examine the following records: [list, with date ranges]. Please let me know a time within ten business days when they will be available. I would also like copies of [items] and understand copies may be charged at no more than 15 cents per page.

Useful records to ask for

  • Annual budget and reserve study
  • Management and vendor contracts
  • Board meeting minutes
  • Your own violation file, including photos and inspection notes
  • Architectural committee decisions on similar requests

Common mistakes

  • Asking for "everything." Broad requests invite delay; be specific.
  • Expecting closed-session records. Those can be withheld under 33-1805(B)(3).
  • No paper trail. A verbal request is hard to prove later.

After you get the records

Compare what you received with what you asked for, and send a short written note listing anything missing. Keep the records organized by date. They often become the exhibits for a written reply or petition later.

If you live in a condo

A.R.S. 33-1258 gives condo unit owners the same rights: free review, 10 business days to examine or receive copies, copies at no more than 15 cents per page, and the same categories that may be withheld. It does not apply to timeshare plan associations under chapter 20 (33-1258(D)). Cite 33-1258 instead of 33-1805 in your request.

FAQ

Does my Arizona HOA have to let me see its records?

Yes, financial and other records must be reasonably available for member examination, with listed exceptions (A.R.S. 33-1805).

How long does an HOA have to provide records?

10 business days, for examination or copies (A.R.S. 33-1805(A)).

Can the HOA charge me?

Not for review. Copies may cost no more than 15 cents per page (A.R.S. 33-1805(A)).

What records can be kept private?

Attorney-privileged communications, pending litigation, closed-session minutes, and certain personal or employee records (A.R.S. 33-1805(B)).
