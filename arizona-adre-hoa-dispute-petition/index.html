Filing an HOA Dispute Petition with the Arizona Department of Real Estate

Most HOA disputes end with a calm written exchange. When one doesn't, Arizona offers an administrative hearing process through the Arizona Department of Real Estate (ADRE).

Who can petition

For a dispute between an owner and a condominium or planned community association regulated under Title 33, chapter 9 or 16, the owner or the association may petition ADRE for a hearing about violations of the community documents or of the condo/planned community statutes (A.R.S. 32-2199.01(A)).

What ADRE can't hear

  • Disputes among owners where the association isn't a party.
  • Disputes with a developer, builder, or seller about the design, construction, condition, or sale of property. (A.R.S. 32-2199.01(A))

Filing

The petition must be in writing on a department-approved form, list the complaints, be signed with addresses, state that a hearing is desired, and be filed with the department, along with a filing fee set by the commissioner (A.R.S. 32-2199.01(A),(B)). Check ADRE's website for the current form and fee. If you withdraw before a hearing is scheduled, or the parties stipulate to dismissal before then, the fee is refunded.

What happens next

ADRE mails a copy to the HOA by certified mail; the HOA must respond within 20 days after mailing, showing cause why the petition should be dismissed. The commissioner then reviews it and, if justified, refers it to the Office of Administrative Hearings, or may dismiss it (A.R.S. 32-2199.01(C),(D)).

Connection to violation letters

Under A.R.S. 33-1803(E), a member may petition at any time, before or after the violation-letter information exchange, if the dispute is within ADRE's jurisdiction.

Before you file

Gather the notice, your certified reply, the HOA's response, records you requested, photos, and a timeline. A clear paper trail makes your petition easier to follow.

Step by step

  1. Confirm jurisdiction: an owner vs. the association, about documents or HOA statutes.
  2. Try the written process first, such as a 33-1803 reply or a records request.
  3. Organize your evidence in date order.
  4. Get the current petition form and fee from the Arizona Department of Real Estate.
  5. List each complaint separately, citing the specific rule or statute.
  6. File, then calendar the association's 20-day response period.

Sample complaint wording

Issue 1: The association did not provide records I requested in writing on [date] within ten business days, as required by A.R.S. 33-1805(A). Attached: my request and proof of delivery.

Common mistakes

  • Filing about a neighbor when the association isn't a party.
  • Bundling everything into one vague complaint. Separate, specific issues are easier to follow.
  • Bringing construction-defect claims against a builder; those are outside ADRE's jurisdiction.

What a hearing is like

A petition that is referred goes to the Office of Administrative Hearings. Expect a formal but plain process where each side presents documents and explains its position. Clear, organized exhibits help: a one-page timeline, the relevant rule or statute, and the letters you exchanged. Stick to the issues listed in your petition.

If you live in a condo

ADRE petitions cover condominium associations regulated under Title 33, chapter 9, as well as planned communities under chapter 16 (A.R.S. 32-2199.01(A)). The condo violation-letter statute also says a unit owner may petition at any time before or after the information exchange (A.R.S. 33-1242(D)).

FAQ

Can I file a complaint against my HOA with the state of Arizona?

Owners may petition ADRE for a hearing on violations of community documents or HOA statutes (A.R.S. 32-2199.01).

Does ADRE handle disputes with neighbors?

Not disputes between owners where the association isn't a party (A.R.S. 32-2199.01(A)(1)).

Is there a filing fee?

Yes, set by the commissioner and refundable in limited cases; check ADRE for the current amount (A.R.S. 32-2199.01(A)).

How long does the HOA have to respond?

20 days after the petition is mailed (A.R.S. 32-2199.01(C)).
